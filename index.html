02 • Problem Context

Introduction & Campus Context

Bridging the operational gap between static mess cooking rules and fluctuating student dining turnout.

⚠️

The Campus Food Wastage Reality

  • Informal Rule-of-Thumb Cooking: Mess kitchens prepare fixed batches based on gut feeling and total registered residents (~700–900 students).
  • High Turnout Volatility: Actual attendance fluctuates heavily by 25%–50% due to exams, weekends, campus fests, weather, and menu popularity (e.g., Paneer vs. Lauki).
  • Perishable Leftovers Discarded: Highly perishable cooked dishes cannot be safely stored across shifts and are thrown out, wasting significant budget and kitchen resources daily.
🎯

Presentation & Project Objectives

  • Item-Level Decision Support: Recommend exact preparation quantities per menu item (in kg, liters, standard degh/vessels) rather than just total heads.
  • Empirical Prospective Log: Build a real dataset with mess staff starting August 2026, recording daily plate counts, leftover quantities, and shortages.
  • Practical Kitchen Constraints: Account for minimum cooking batch sizes, portion yields, and the asymmetric cost of food shortage vs. surplus.
  • Validate with Real Operational Baselines: Compare against current manager estimates, 7-day moving averages, and same-weekday historical figures.
Key Insight from Mess Manager Interview

“We don't need a zero-waste illusion or a student voting app. We need simple, explainable daily batch recommendations so the head cook knows whether to prepare 45 kg or 65 kg before lighting the stove.”

03 • Scope & Boundaries

Project Scope & Deliverables

Clearly defining what Mestimator delivers for the 17-week semester, with strict operational boundaries.

✅

In-Scope Core Deliverables

  • Responsive Kitchen PWA: Mobile/tablet interface for mess managers to view recommendations, record overrides, and log leftover weights.
  • Multi-Factor Forecasting Engine: Predicts meal turnout and dish-level demand using day-of-week, meal type, academic calendar flags, and historical dish ratios.
  • Portion & Batch Recipe Optimizer: Translates predicted eaters into kitchen batch units (e.g., kg of Rice, Liters of Dal, trays, pieces).
  • Uncertainty Risk Margin: Displays confidence bounds (e.g., 520 ± 40 diners) and guards against dining stockouts.
  • Multi-Mess Configuration: Configurable dining schedules, menus, vessel sizes, and recipe yields for any hostel or canteen.
  • Prospective Campus Dataset & Audit Trail: Continuous multi-week log of planned vs. actual food outcomes.
🚫

Explicit Non-Goals (Out of Scope)

  • NO Medicine or Multi-Commodity Sharing: Strictly removed from original MediMatch concept due to safety/regulatory limits.
  • NO Open Student-to-Student Redistribution: Mess food cannot be redistributed informally without institutional FSSAI food safety clearance.
  • NO Mandatory Student Voting Foundation: Student preferences are an optional auxiliary signal; system does not rely on student survey compliance.
  • NO Automated Hardware Kitchen Control: Mestimator is a decision-support advisory system; kitchen staff always retain final override authority.
  • NO Black-box LLM Wrapper Forecasting: Uses statistical & ML models (Holt-Winters, Gradient Boosted Trees); LLM is only used for plain-language summaries.

06 • Architecture

System Features & Decision Engine

A multi-stage mathematical decision model translating uncertain turnout into practical kitchen batches.

1. Turnout Forecasting Model

Estimates total plate count $\hat{A}_m$ for meal $m$ based on feature vector:

Features: [Weekday, MealType, ExamFlag, HolidayFlag, Rain/Weather, PrevWeekAttendance]

Compares Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost) vs. 7-Day Seasonal Moving Average.

2. Dish-Level Preference Matrix

Distributes predicted diners into individual dish demand $\hat{D}_{m,i}$ using historical dish substitution weights:

Demand $\hat{D}_{m,i} = \hat{A}_m \times \omega_{i} \times \alpha_{\text{preference}}$

3. Recipe Yield & Batch Quantization

Converts diner demand into physical cooking batches under discrete vessel constraints:

Quantity $Q_i = \text{BatchRound}\left( \hat{D}_{m,i} \times \text{PortionSize}_i + \text{SafetyBuffer}_i \right)$

Ensures food is cooked in realistic batch increments (e.g. 5kg dal pots or 20L kettles).

4. Asymmetric Risk Matrix

Food shortages are operationally worse than minor surplus. The loss function assigns a higher penalty to under-preparation:

Penalty: $L(Q) = c_{\text{waste}} \cdot \max(0, Q - D) + 3.5 \cdot c_{\text{shortage}} \cdot \max(0, D - Q)$
Multi-Mess SaaS Configuration: Configurable per hostel mess for dining schedules (Breakfast/Lunch/Dinner/Snacks), custom weekly menus, standardized container capacities, and dish portion benchmarks.

08 • Non-Functional Requirements

Performance, Security & Quality Attributes

Engineered for rock-solid reliability, swift responsiveness in the kitchen, and zero privacy compromise.

⚡

Performance Goals

  • Recommendation Latency: < 400 ms on mobile devices for batch calculations.
  • Offline Availability: 100% core calculation functions operate even if campus internet drops during kitchen hours.
  • Data Sync Window: Background sync completes within 3 seconds of network recovery.
  • Uptime Target: 99.9% availability during active preparation hours (6:00 AM – 9:00 PM).
🔒

Security & Privacy Measures

  • Zero Student PII Collection: System strictly processes aggregate plate counts and food weights; no student biometric or ID data stored.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC):
    • Mess Manager: Full edit & override permissions.
    • Kitchen Staff: View-only recipe quantities & log leftover counts.
    • Warden: Read-only analytics reports.
  • Audit Logging: Immutable record for every manual recommendation override with mandatory reason code.
🛡️

Software Quality Attributes

  • High Usability: Large tap targets (min 48px) and high-contrast color scheme for kitchen environments.
  • Fault Tolerance: If forecasting engine fails or has missing inputs, system automatically falls back to safe 14-day median rolling average.
  • Maintainability: Modular decoupled architecture (Forecasting Engine separated from UI & Database Layer).
