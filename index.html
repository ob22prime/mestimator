01 • Problem

What Is the Problem?

Hostel mess cooks too much food every day because they don't know how many students will show up.

The Problem Today

  • Blind cooking: Kitchen makes food for ~800 students every meal, but only 500 to 650 actually show up.
  • Attendance changes a lot: Weekends, exams, festivals, popular vs unpopular dishes all change how many students eat.
  • Food gets thrown away: Cooked food can't be stored. Whatever is extra goes to waste every single day.

What Our Project Does

  • Predicts how many students will eat based on day, exams, and which dish is on the menu.
  • Tells the cook exact amounts like "Make 70 kg Paneer, 90 L Dal, 1400 Rotis" instead of guessing.
  • Adds a small safety buffer so food never runs out and waste is reduced significantly.
  • We are collecting real data from the hostel mess since August 2026 to test this.
What the Mess Manager Told Us

“We don't need a fancy student voting app. Just tell us whether to cook 45 kg or 65 kg before we light the stove.”

02 • Scope

What We Will Build

What our project delivers this semester.

Project Deliverables

  • A simple web app that works on any phone or tablet in the kitchen.
  • A prediction system that guesses how many students will eat each meal using past data, day of week, exams, and dish popularity.
  • Cooking quantity calculator that converts student count into kg, liters, and number of pots to cook.
  • Safety margin so food never runs out (e.g. "560 students expected, give or take 35").
  • Works for any hostel by changing the menu, pot sizes, and meal timings.
  • Works offline so the kitchen can use it even when Wi-Fi is down.

05 • Core Logic

How the Prediction Works

4 simple steps to go from "800 registered students" to "Cook 70 kg Paneer."

Step 1: Guess How Many Will Eat

Looks at what day it is, if there are exams, and last week's numbers to predict attendance.

Inputs: Day of Week, Meal Time, Exams, Last Week's Attendance

Step 2: Split by Dish Popularity

If Paneer is served, more students eat. If Lauki is served, fewer show up. We use past data to know this.

Example: 70% take Paneer, 85% take Dal, only 45% take Lauki

Step 3: Convert to Cooking Amounts

Turns the student count into actual kitchen numbers the cook understands.

Example: 560 students eating Paneer = 70 kg = 2 big pots

Rounds up to real pot sizes used in the kitchen (35 kg pots, 20 kg pots, etc).

Step 4: Add Safety Buffer

We always add a little extra (~6.5%) so food never runs out. Running out of food is worse than having a little extra.

Better to have 5 kg extra than 50 students with no food
Works for Any Hostel: Just change the menu, pot sizes, and meal times to use it in a different hostel.

07 • Quality

Speed, Privacy & Reliability

Making sure the app is fast, safe, and always works when the kitchen needs it.

Fast & Reliable

  • Quick Results: Cooking suggestions appear in under half a second.
  • Works Offline: Kitchen Wi-Fi goes down often, the app still works completely.
  • Auto Sync: When internet comes back, data syncs automatically.
  • Always Available: Works during all meal hours (6 AM to 9 PM).

Privacy & Access Control

  • No Student Data Stored: We only save total plate counts and food weights. No names, no roll numbers, no photos.
  • Who Can Do What:
    • Manager: Can edit and change suggestions.
    • Cook: Can view amounts and enter leftovers.
    • Warden: Can only read weekly reports.
  • Change Log: Every time someone changes a suggestion, the reason is saved.

Easy to Use & Safe Fallback

  • Big Buttons: Easy to tap even with wet or oily hands in the kitchen.
  • Safe Default: If something goes wrong, the app uses the average of the last 14 days as a safe guess.
  • Clean Code: Prediction logic, screen, and data storage are kept separate so it's easy to update.

09 • Summary

Conclusion & References

How we will measure success and the research papers behind our approach.

How We Compare Against Current Method
Method What It Does How Good?
Current Way Manager guesses based on experience Often wrong, lots of waste
Last Week Same Day Cook same as last Monday for this Monday Better, but ignores exams and dish changes
7-Day Average Average of last 7 days Smoother, but still misses patterns
Mestimator (Ours) Uses day, exams, and dish popularity together Less waste, no one goes hungry
How We Measure Success
  • Prediction Accuracy: How close our guess is to the actual number of students who ate.
  • Waste Reduction: How many kg of food we saved compared to the old method.
  • No Shortages: Students should never go hungry because we undercooked.
  • Cook Trusts It: The mess manager follows our suggestion at least 80% of the time.
